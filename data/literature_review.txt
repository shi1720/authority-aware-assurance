Closest antecedents:
1. Alpos and Cachin (SSS 2023 / ePrint 2022/1767): already implements Boolean access structures and argues for correlated-failure models. This invalidates any novelty claim for replacing simple thresholds with Boolean trust rules. The paper explicitly credits this work.
2. Malkhi and Reiter (1998), Alpos et al. (2024): prior availability/integrity and general/asymmetric trust. Our separation lemma is elementary; we do not claim a new consensus construction.
3. Wasil et al. (2024), Harack et al. (2025), Baker et al. (2025): prior international AI verification, multi-layer and multi-jurisdiction designs, vendor distrust, and approval allocation. Harack PDF page 67 and Baker PDF page 20 were checked for close mechanisms. Our contribution is the bounded combination of role audit, separate capture/denial maps, failure witness and U.S. partnership application.
4. RFC 9334: already separates verification roles. We apply, rather than invent, these roles.
5. OVERT 1.0 ATT-5.3: already proposes notary independence across ownership, hosting and jurisdiction. We do not claim discovery of notary diversification.
6. Farrell and Newman (2019): network concentration/jurisdiction as a source of state power. Our policy hypothesis is an application to certification dependence; it is not evidence of actual coercion by NVIDIA or the U.S.
